## 11. Current provisional product definition — H2

### 11.1 Decision summary, evidence, and confidence

**Decision.** Provisionally define an **Authorized Release Record**: a small release receipt used beside the existing proofing and social-publishing stack. Begin as a template/checklist, not software.

- **Fact — high confidence:** official incumbent documentation establishes separate proof/version and social approval/publishing layers.
- **Inference — low confidence:** continuity between those layers may require manual reconciliation.
- **Hypothesis — low confidence:** a compact release record may reduce ambiguity about what was approved and what may be published.
- **Unknown:** real recurrence, measurable consequence, user value, adoption, buyer, budget, and willingness to pay.

The definition is “good enough” for a reversible comparison, not evidence of a validated product.

### 11.2 Target segment, user, job, and handoff

- **Hypothesis:** target segment — creative agencies repeatedly producing client-facing short-form branded video and social variants.
- **Hypothesis:** primary user — social/content lead or account/client-service lead responsible for release coordination.
- **Hypothesis:** recurring job — ensure that the precise approved asset and caption are the ones an authorized person schedules and publishes.
- **Exact handoff:** `proof decision → locked asset/version + exact caption/channel → named publishing authority → scheduled-post identifier → published-post link`.

### 11.3 Problem statement and consequence

**Hypothesis:** when proofing and publishing occur in different systems, the release actor may need to reconstruct the approved version, caption, channel, and authority from filenames, links, chat, or memory.

**Unknown:** whether this causes wrong-version posts, caption swaps, manual checking, chase time, delay, missed slots, or material cost in the target segment. No consequence is claimed as observed.

### 11.4 Current stack, workaround, alternatives, and insufficiency

**Fact:** Frame.io/Ziflow document version-specific proofing and decision records; Canva/Sprout document approvals, permissions, scheduling, imports, and publishing. **Assumption:** teams may bridge them with filenames, shared links, chat/email, calendars, and manual checks. Actual target-team configurations are **Unknown**.

| Option | Minimum comparison | Decision rule |
|---|---|---|
| Existing stack configuration | Use native asset, approval, permission, scheduling, and final-post fields as one source of truth. | Prefer it if it produces the same unambiguous trace. |
| Process/SLA change — H7 | Named approver/delegate, decision deadline, revision cap, reminder, and escalation. | Prefer it if the issue is decision delay rather than release-state continuity. |
| Contract or approval-rule change | Define who approves, who may publish, what counts as a material change, and when reapproval is required. | Prefer it when authority—not missing state—is the root issue. |
| Template/checklist | Authorized Release Record containing only the minimum fields below. | **Selected first test shape.** |
| Integration/connector | Transfer identifiers/status between two named systems. | Consider only after a real surviving re-entry failure; not currently authorized. |

### 11.7 Smallest useful shape and minimum boundary

The smallest useful shape is a **template/checklist**, with one row or card per intended post:

1. release-record ID;
2. proof link and locked asset/version ID;
3. exact caption revision and channel;
4. creative decision, named approver, and timestamp;
5. named publishing authority and account;
6. scheduled-post ID/time;
7. published-post link or exception state;
8. separate clearance links only when applicable.

No files, credentials, biometric data, contracts, claim evidence, or rights records are duplicated into it. A connector or companion tool is outside the current boundary.

### 11.8 Explicit non-goals and boundaries

This is not a scheduler, proofing suite, DAM, creative generator, approval system, rights registry, legal-clearance system, claims verifier, provenance validator, disclosure engine, generic project manager, or all-in-one agency platform. It does not grant approval, rights, account permission, or publishing authority. It does not define architecture, a backlog, or a roadmap.

### 11.9 First useful outcome without a complete product

Run a tabletop comparison using three synthetic scenarios: normal approval; caption changed after approval; asset version swapped before scheduling. Complete each scenario using (a) native incumbent fields, (b) H7 plus a release checklist, and (c) the Authorized Release Record.

### 11.10 Adoption, trust, privacy, security, rights, permissions, and integration constraints

- **Assumption:** adoption requires adding less work than the manual checks it replaces; the acceptable burden is unverified.
- Use links and identifiers rather than copying sensitive assets or evidence.
- Do not store credentials or convert a person's name into authority without an explicit role assignment.
- Preserve least-privilege access and the source systems' permissions.
- Treat rights, claims, provenance, disclosure, and publication authority as independent decisions.
- **Unknown:** target systems, plans, APIs, authentication, retention rules, data residency, security review, and integration access.

### 11.11 Operating model and responsibilities

- **Assumption:** the account/social lead creates the release record.
- The creative approver confirms the exact version and caption; this does not grant publishing permission.
- The publishing authority confirms channel/account/time and records the scheduled and final links.
- Legal, brand, or rightsholders own their separate decisions where required.
- The agency operations owner maintains the template/rule and retires it if native configuration is sufficient.

### 11.12 Success measures, failure criteria, and disconfirmation

For the synthetic comparison only, success means all three scenarios preserve an unambiguous link between approved version/caption, authority, scheduled post, and final post, while exposing the deliberate swap/change cases. Time and field-count should be recorded, not pre-claimed.

Kill the concept when:

- native social-system configuration produces the same trace with equal or lower effort;
- H7 or a contract/approval rule resolves the scenario;
- the record duplicates another source of truth or increases stale/conflicting state;
- useful operation requires copied sensitive data, credentials, or broad permissions;
- the concept cannot later be tested on permissioned real work; or
- no real recurrence, consequence, reachable role, or economic path emerges if primary evidence is ever sought.

**Disconfirmation:** H2 closes if the configured incumbent reliably binds the exact approved asset/version, caption, authority, scheduled post, and final post. H7 disconfirms a software premise when named ownership, delegation, SLA, revision cap, reminders, and escalation address the failure.
